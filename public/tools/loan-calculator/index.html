    🎯 How Calculations Work

    Monthly Payment: Using amortization formula to split payment between principal and interest. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal.

    Total Interest: Sum of all interest paid over loan life. Longer terms = more interest, even if monthly payment is lower.

    💡 How to Use Results

    • Monthly Payment: Budget this amount each month
    • Total Interest: Shows true cost of borrowing. A $300,000 house might cost $500,000+ with interest
    • Amortization Schedule: See how each payment splits between principal and interest
    • Early Payoff: See how much interest you'd save by paying extra

    🔄 Decision Scenarios

    • Shorter vs Longer Term: 15-year mortgage saves ~60% interest vs 30-year, but monthly payment is higher
    • Extra Principal Payments: Pay $100 extra/month to save years and thousands in interest
    • Lower Rate Impact: 0.5% rate difference = $10,000s saved on large loans
    • Refinancing: If rates drop, refinancing can save substantial interest

    📊 Typical Interest Rates (2024)

    • 30-year mortgage: 6-7%
    • 15-year mortgage: 5.5-6.5%
    • Auto loan (new): 5-8%
    • Auto loan (used): 6-10%
    • Personal loan: 8-36%

    ⚠️ Important Notes

    • This does NOT include property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees (mortgage)
    • This does NOT include car insurance, maintenance (auto loans)
    • Real rate depends on credit score—higher scores get better rates
    • Consider making extra payments to reduce total interest paid

    📋 Understanding Your Inputs

    • Loan Amount: Total principal borrowed
    • Annual Interest Rate: Yearly rate (%), varies by credit score and loan type
    • Loan Term: Years to repay (e.g., 30 for mortgages, 5 for cars)

    💡 Quick Tips

    • Shorter terms save interest but require higher monthly payments
    • Even small rate differences compound to big savings
    • Extra principal payments dramatically reduce total interest
